# Composition pattern catalog

Use this catalog after the selector chooses rich mode. Start with the smallest pattern that expresses the answer. The examples are structural sketches; complete machine-readable specs live in `examples/golden/`.

## Advanced patterns

### hypertext-journal

Use for a long article with several reading routes. Good: a three-item anchor index, a visible thesis, the first chapter open, two closed chapters, and sources last. Bad: ten anchor links before the reader knows the subject, or anchors separated from their visible headings.

### museum-drawers

Use for an object, place, exhibit, or product that benefits from layered inspection. Good: one hero, compact identity facts, and drawers for provenance and sound. Bad: a hero map, slideshow, collage, and audio all competing above the fold.

### marked-second-reading

Use when a few marked words form a legitimate secondary phrase or state sequence. Good: three marked state words across three short sentences. Bad: marking whole sentences or hiding a claim only in the secondary reading.

### interactive-redaction

Use for a coordinated reveal. Good: one text spoiler and one related media spoiler with the warning still visible. Bad: concealing the answer, safety advice, caption credit, or the entire composition.

### ticket-table

Use for a passport, coordinate plate, mini-calendar, comparison ticket, or compact grid. Good: two short mobile columns with meaningful alignment. Bad: six prose-heavy columns or layout padding made from spaces.

### map-cover

Use when place is the primary subject. Good: one map, one coordinate line, and a short interpretation. Bad: a map plus another hero visual and several long paragraphs before the location is explained.

### code-typography

Use a short code block as machine voice, metadata, or programmatic epigraph. Good: three concise lines followed by a plain-language explanation. Bad: decorative pseudo-code, a large log dump, or space-aligned artwork.

### manual-animation

Use a slideshow when swiping expresses time or successive states. Good: four consistently framed stages of one process. Bad: unrelated images, or using a slideshow for values that must be compared simultaneously.

### hidden-sound-note

Use for optional audio that should not dominate the card. Good: a closed details block labeled with subject and duration. Bad: unlabeled audio, or nested media when the adapter does not support it.

### title-pullquote

Use for a poster-like phrase with provenance. Good: one short line and a compact source or issue credit. Bad: a paragraph-sized quote, multiple competing quotes, or decorative attribution.

### emoji-glyph-system

Use custom emoji as a consistent category or issue notation. Good: one pack and two repeated semantic glyphs. Bad: mixing unrelated packs or using glyphs as confetti.

### second-narrator-notes

Use references to add doubt, provenance, alternate accounts, or technical context. Good: two short notes linked exactly where needed. Bad: footnoting every sentence or moving the main conclusion into a note.

## High-level presets

### issue

For an editorial article or research note. Good: indexed title, restrained anchors, three to five chapters, and references. Bad: decorating a two-sentence reply as a magazine issue.

### artifact

For an object, location, product, or observation. Good: one hero, a compact facts table, metadata, and provenance. Bad: multiple heroes and no clear object identity.

### scene

For a visual story or diagram sequence. Good: a three-to-five-frame slideshow, one pullquote, and closed location or credit details. Bad: a slideshow with one image or frames that have no order.

## Selection shorthand

| Need | Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| One fact or confirmation | Plain text | Any showcase pattern |
| Simultaneous visual comparison | Collage | Slideshow |
| Ordered visual states | Slideshow | Collage |
| Secondary evidence | Closed details | Hiding the answer |
| Real user decision | Buttons | Decorative buttons |
| Dense values | Two-column table | Prose-heavy wide table |

Validate every result. A visually appropriate pattern can still be unsafe or unsupported on the active adapter.
